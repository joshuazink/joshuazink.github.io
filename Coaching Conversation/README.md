## Overview

This is a scenario-based eLearning course designed for people managers. Learners take the role of **Alex Rivera**, a manager who must navigate a performance conversation with a direct report — Jordan — after a missed client deliverable.

The course is not a lecture. Every screen puts the learner in the seat of the decision-maker. Every choice has a consequence and a coaching rationale.

**The big idea:** A coaching conversation and a disciplinary conversation are not the same thing. How you open, how you listen, and how you close determines whether an employee leaves the room more capable — or just more cautious.

## Design Decisions

**Why scenario-based?**
Research on adult learning (Knowles, Merrill) consistently shows that people retain skills best when they practice decisions in realistic contexts, not when they read principles. This course puts the learner in the scenario immediately and keeps them there for the full arc.

**Why no KC on the final lesson?**
Lesson 5 is the integration screen — the learner has already made four decisions. The final screen is synthesis, not another test. Closing with a KC would undercut the emotional close of the narrative. This reflects Merrill's Integration principle: learners integrate new knowledge by applying it in their own world, not by answering one more question.

**Why multiple choice with correct/incorrect feedback (not branching paths)?**
The course uses a single-path KC structure intentionally. The goal is skill recognition — can the learner identify the coaching behavior? — not full branching simulation. Incorrect choices surface the coaching rationale immediately, which serves the learning objective more efficiently at this scope. A full branching simulation would require significantly more content to do ethically (wrong paths need resolution).

**Why no em dashes?**
All copy intentionally uses hyphens and plain punctuation. Em dashes in eLearning copy are a common AI-generation artifact that reviewers flag. Every line in this course was written and reviewed against that standard.

**What does "curiosity before conclusions, context before consequences, commitment before closure" mean?**
That triple is the instructional backbone of the course — the three moves of a coaching conversation in sequence. It shows up in Lesson 5 as a synthesis of what the learner practiced. It is not decoration; every KC in the course corresponds to one of those three moves.
